# Phase 1 gate: freezing the eval bed before any agent exists

*2026-08-04 · ai-portfolio-sentinel · learning lane*

Phase 1 of the sentinel closed today by freezing the thing most
monitoring projects build last: the evidence standard the system will
be judged against. No checker exists yet — not one line of agent code,
no control plane, no scheduler. What exists is the cage the future
system must pass through, committed before the system can argue with
it. Gate frozen at `4d46c1d4fc3c4f485a83f44fa54afa6b04b1f541`.

The frozen bed is eight synthetic repository snapshots (labeled
synthetic on every page) carrying sixty injected defects — exactly ten
per check class across six classes: broken links, number mismatches,
stale state markers, missing required files, missing synthetic labels,
and README structure violations. Alongside them, 166 enumerated clean
units stand as distractors. Every injection wrote its ground-truth row
at injection time; nothing was reconstructed from memory afterward.

Two things happened during the freeze that are worth recording
honestly. First, the planned corpus was infeasible: ten structure
defects across four READMEs cannot yield unique finding locations
under the frozen location semantics (a removed header maps to the
nearest surviving one — at most two removals per README stay
distinguishable). The fix was an owner ruling, not a quiet fudge: two
extra all-clean snapshots, structure defects spread across six
READMEs, every total preserved. Second, the review protocol's own
wording overstated itself. "Blind labeling" implied the reviewer
discovers defect classes cold; in truth the packet shape can reveal
the candidate class. The governing documents now say exactly what the
review certifies — defect presence and location, independently
identified — and nothing more.

The thresholds are frozen as ratios and as integers, because a ratio
whose integer meaning is unstated is not frozen. Pooled recall 0.85
at sixty positives means nine misses; per-class recall 0.80 at ten
cases means two misses, and a third fails the class. Precision 0.90
binds over whatever the system actually emits. The false-flag ceiling
works out to sixteen of the 166 clean units — a count that itself got
corrected at the owner checkpoint, when a reconciliation demand
exposed eight clean units a lazy enumeration rule had silently
dropped. All arithmetic is decimal, checked in CI against the
committed corpus counts — the config cannot silently drift from the
data.

An external model reviewed a forty-percent sample of the positives
plus clean controls through deterministic, seeded packets; every
disagreement was adjudicated by the operator and the record is
committed with the key. The blind-packet generator reproduces its
output byte-identically from the committed seed.

What was learned: eval engineering for long-horizon systems is mostly
the discipline of making future failure arguable in advance —
quantized thresholds nobody can renegotiate after a run, location
semantics precise enough to make "found it" a checkable claim, and a
corpus whose own construction is tested. The eval gate runs on
synthetic fixtures with a frozen answer key; live runs, when they
come, are real data against my own repositories — the two are labeled
wherever results appear, and neither borrows the other's credibility.

Next: Phase 2 — the deterministic control plane, run against this bed,
with zero LLM calls.
